Response to Reviewers
Dear Editor and Reviewers,
We thank you for the careful evaluation of our manuscript and for the constructive comments 
that have improved its clarity, rigor, and reproducibility. Below we provide a point-by-point 
summary of the revisions made. For clarity, reviewer comments are paraphrased in italics, 
followed by our responses.
Reviewer 1
**Comment 1. The machine learning framework requires clearer justification and improved 
robustness, particularly regarding class imbalance and model stability. **
Response: We substantially revised the Random Forest (RF) workflow to address these 
concerns:
 The number of trees was increased from 300 to 500 to improve model stability.
 Class imbalance was explicitly addressed by capping the number of training samples at 
50,000 observations per class.
 Importantly, this sampling is applied after the train/test split, ensuring that validation 
metrics reflect the original class distribution.
 Leave-one-out (LOO) validation models were updated to use the same configuration (500 
trees; 50,000 samples per class), ensuring consistency between validation and final model 
outputs.
The Methods section has been revised to state:
“To reduce class imbalance while maintaining sufficient representation of dominant classes, the 
training dataset was balanced by randomly sampling up to 50,000 observations per class. 
Random forest models were trained using 500 trees.”
**Comment 2. The observed model artifacts suggest potential issues with model configuration 
or training data. **
Response: We clarified that the previously observed artifacts were primarily due to class 
imbalance, not insufficient model complexity. The revised manuscript now emphasizes that 
training data composition is the dominant control on model behavior, whereas increasing the 
number of trees alone does not resolve bias.

Reviewer 2
**Comment 3. The unsupervised clustering approach requires clearer justification and 
methodological transparency. **
Response: The K-means clustering workflow was revised as follows:
 The number of clusters was standardized to k = 6 for both PlanetScope and Landsat 
datasets, based on combined evaluation using elbow and silhouette methods.
 The clustering function was improved to remove reliance on global variables and to 
ensure consistent handling of raster structure.
 NA handling was refined to operate on a per-date basis, preventing unnecessary loss of 
valid observations.
The manuscript now clearly describes K-means as a step to partition spectral space prior to 
supervised classification.
**Comment 4. Geospatial preprocessing and data provenance require additional clarification. 
**
Response: We expanded the preprocessing section to include:
 Explicit identification of data levels:
o PlanetScope imagery as surface reflectance (Level 3B)
o Landsat imagery as Collection 2 Level-2 surface reflectance
 Clarification of temporal alignment between datasets.
 Documentation of a second set of PlanetScope dates used for mesohabitat comparison.
 Improved description of raster stacking and band standardization procedures.
Reviewer 3
**Comment 5. The mesohabitat classification workflow requires clearer methodological 
justification and improved efficiency. **
Response: We revised the mesohabitat workflow to improve both interpretability and 
performance:
 Mesohabitat classes are now derived directly from depth and velocity rasters using a 
consistent classification function.
 Median conditions are computed from median depth and velocity, rather than from 
classified rasters.

 Raster processing was optimized to reduce redundant reprojection and resampling 
operations.
 A revised color scheme was implemented to improve interpretability, with pools 
represented by blue gradients and high-velocity environments by warm colors.
Reviewer 4
**Comment 6. Statistical interpretation requires clarification, particularly regarding 
correlations between land cover classes. **
Response: The statistical analysis section was revised to:
 Clearly distinguish between moderate negative correlations observed in semi-supervised 
models and weak relationships in supervised models.
 Provide explicit geomorphic interpretation linking these relationships to river dynamics 
(submergence vs exposure).
 Improve explanation of differences between PlanetScope and Landsat results in terms of 
spatial resolution and sensitivity to fine-scale features.
Reviewer 5
**Comment 7. Figures require improvement in clarity, layout, and interpretability. **
Response: All figures were redesigned to improve visual quality and communication:
 The study area figure was completely restructured using a hierarchical layout with a 
dominant study-reach panel and supporting locator maps.
 Time series figures were consolidated to reduce redundancy and improve readability.
 Model comparison figures were refined with improved legend placement, consistent 
panel sizing, and reduced clutter.
